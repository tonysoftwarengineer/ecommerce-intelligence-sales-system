PURPOSE

Evolve the Ecommerce Intelligence Sales System from a trusted analytics
dashboard into an evidence-backed decision-intelligence product for small
businesses. The system must explain material business changes, identify risks,
state its confidence and limitations, and suggest safe next actions.

The project is deliberately built as a learn-as-we-go AI engineering project.
Before each milestone, record the problem, the options, the selected design,
and the trade-offs. After each milestone, add tests and record what should be
revisited later.

FIRST-RELEASE AUDIENCE DECISION (2026-09-21)

The first portfolio release is designed for small online retailers selling
physical products with repeat sales. Lead with validated sales changes and
safe investigation prompts. Seven-day product-unit demand is an optional,
clearly labelled planning preview. Shipping, refund, and catalogue documents
may support separately cited questions through the bounded RAG feature.

The engine still accepts other sales CSVs that meet its existing field and
business-rule requirements; there is no industry declaration or hard gate.
The sales path needs order ID, order date, customer ID, and a valid revenue
representation. Product demand additionally needs reliable product identity,
quantity, units, adequate safe history, and truthful open-day and stockout
confirmations. Missing evidence produces a correction reason or unavailable
result, not an invented forecast.

The next forecasting checkpoint is evidence collection for this retailer
audience. Existing development comparison and fresh M5 validation rejected the
tested 10% sparse-abstention rule; a three-block consistency candidate also
failed development screening. Preserve those results. Do not adjust their
thresholds and retest the same cohorts as fresh proof. Obtain an independent
permissioned retailer export with stockout and product-lifecycle semantics,
then predeclare any new safeguard and test it on untouched future weeks.
See docs/evaluation/online_retailer_evidence_matrix.md for current coverage.

PHASE GOAL: DIAGNOSTIC INTELLIGENCE

When a user uploads a supported sales export, the system will answer:
1. What changed?
2. Which measurable factors contributed to the change?
3. Is the change unusual relative to available history?
4. How confident is the system, and what data limits apply?
5. What should the business investigate or do next?

The intended business outcome is a decision-ready report for a weekly or
monthly operating review, not an autonomous system that makes financial or
commercial decisions on behalf of the business.

MILESTONE 1: PRODUCT CONTRACT AND GUARDRAILS

Objective

Define exactly what the first Diagnostic Intelligence release is permitted to
say, the evidence it must show, and the conditions that disable a diagnostic.

In scope

- Revenue, completed-order, average-order-value, customer, category, regional,
  discount, refund, pending-value, and payment diagnostics where the source
  data supports them.
- Comparison of complete periods only.
- Explicit confidence and limitation labels.
- Deterministic, evidence-backed findings and suggested investigations.

Out of scope for this milestone

- LLM-generated findings or recommendations.
- RAG retrieval.
- Causal claims about pricing, marketing, or operational changes.
- Inventory, profit, churn-probability, or stockout recommendations without
  their required source fields and evaluated models.
- Authentication, durable storage, and multi-tenant deployment.

REQUIRED OUTPUT CONTRACT

The future diagnostic report will be a typed object, not a free-form text
response. Each insight must eventually contain:

- id: stable identifier for the insight type.
- title: concise statement of the detected condition.
- category: performance_change, anomaly, data_quality, or opportunity.
- observation: factual statement written from validated metrics.
- comparison_period: the exact periods or baseline used.
- evidence: metric names, values, changes, and provenance.
- contributors: factors that mathematically contributed to a change, when
  applicable.
- confidence: high, medium, low, or unavailable.
- limitations: data or model constraints that qualify the finding.
- recommended_action: a safe next investigation or action.
- priority: critical, high, medium, or low.

Facts, interpretations, and recommendations must remain separate fields.
This makes every report auditable and prepares the system for a future LLM
explanation layer without allowing it to invent calculations.

CAPABILITY MATRIX

Diagnostic                                  Minimum required data
------------------------------------------  ---------------------------------
Revenue and order-volume comparison         order_id, order_date, revenue
Average order value comparison               order_id, order_date, revenue
Customer-count comparison                    customer_id, order_date, revenue
Category contribution                        product_category, order_date, revenue
Regional contribution                        state_or_region, order_date, revenue
Discount diagnostic                          discount plus revenue fields
Refund diagnostic                            refund_amount or returned_quantity
Pending/cancellation diagnostic              order_status or payment_status
Payment/chargeback diagnostic                payment status, payment amount, or
                                              chargeback amount as appropriate
Historical anomaly diagnostic                enough complete, continuous periods
Forecast-related diagnostic                  forecast capability already available
Inventory recommendation                     inventory, lead time, and demand data
Profit recommendation                        unit cost and relevant fulfilment costs

If a requirement is not met, the report must state that the diagnostic is
unavailable and explain why. It must never infer missing fields.

NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS

- Deterministic: the same canonical data and configuration produce the same
  diagnostic report.
- Traceable: every numerical statement links to calculated evidence.
- Conservative: limited history, incomplete periods, mixed currencies, and
  ambiguous data reduce or disable conclusions.
- Fast: diagnostics should run synchronously for the current 10 MiB upload
  limit; performance will be benchmarked before any production scale claim.
- Testable: each diagnostic rule has positive, negative, and unavailable-data
  test scenarios.
- Safe: no direct financial, pricing, or inventory action is performed.
- Extensible: the report contract can be persisted and later passed to an LLM
  or RAG layer without changing the financial calculation path.

LOCKED DESIGN DECISIONS

1. Deterministic analytics first
   Python code calculates business metrics, comparisons, and priorities. An
   LLM may later explain a validated report but will not calculate money.

2. Modular monolith for this phase
   Diagnostics will live beside generic_sales as a cohesive module. Separate
   services are not justified until independent scaling or deployment needs
   exist.

3. Capability-aware execution
   Source-data availability controls which diagnostics appear. This follows
   the existing validation philosophy and prevents false confidence.

4. No causal language without causal evidence
   The system may say a factor contributed mathematically or was associated
   with a change. It may not say the factor caused the change.

5. Human-readable confidence rules
   Confidence is a transparent assessment of evidence sufficiency, not an
   invented probability. The rule set will be defined and tested in Milestone 2.

6. RAG is an augmentation, not the calculation engine
   Future retrieval will search business policies, catalogues, supplier terms,
   and historical validated reports. It will not replace structured analysis
   of raw transaction rows.

DEFERRED PRODUCT/UX BACKLOG

Guided diagnostic investigation workflow (not implemented)

The current dashboard presents an evidence-backed finding, its priority score,
and a human-review investigation prompt. It does not yet guide a business
through the investigation inside the product. A future workflow should:

- translate the measured contributors (for example, completed orders and
  average order value) into a short review checklist;
- link each checklist item to only the available supporting breakdowns or
  correction evidence; and
- let the business record a human conclusion without converting that conclusion
  into an unverified causal claim.

This is deferred because it needs a durable, authenticated feedback design and
real-business usability evaluation. Until then, recommendations remain prompts
for human review rather than an in-dashboard investigation workflow.

Product-demand coverage and stockout capture (not implemented)

The current product-demand screen requires a business confirmation that the
export covers every open day and that no stockouts are unrecorded. It can accept
known business-closure dates, but it does not yet provide a low-friction way to
enter known stockout dates by product. A future workflow should support grouped
confirmation, correction guidance, and a structured stockout import or entry
path. Until then, users must leave the stockout confirmation unchecked whenever
known stockout dates cannot be represented safely.

MILESTONE 3 COMPLETION RECORD

Implemented in src/diagnostics/comparison.py with tests in
tests/test_diagnostic_comparison.py.

The first diagnostic calculation compares the two latest complete, consecutive
months of recognized sales. It then decomposes the sales change into the exact
mathematical contribution from:
- completed-order count; and
- average order value.

Decision: use recognized_sales rather than net revenue for this decomposition.
Recognized sales has the exact identity:

    recognized sales = completed orders × average order value

Refund-only adjustments are excluded from this calculation and will receive a
separate diagnostic. This avoids presenting a refund timing effect as a change
in completed-order behaviour.

The order and AOV contributions use a symmetric formula so they always add to
the total sales change and do not depend on calculation order.

Guardrails now enforced by the calculation:
- Only one currency can be analyzed at a time.
- The two periods must be consecutive months.
- An incomplete latest month is excluded.
- Missing months are not assumed to be zero sales.
- Each comparison requires at least one completed order in both periods.
- The output says it is a two-period comparison, not a causal or anomaly claim.

Verification: ruff check, mypy, and the non-integration test suite passed
(140 passed, 4 deselected) on 2026-08-04.

MILESTONE 4 COMPLETION RECORD

Implemented in src/diagnostics/anomaly.py with tests in
tests/test_diagnostic_anomaly.py.

The anomaly detector evaluates the latest complete month against the rolling
median and Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) of the six prior complete,
consecutive months. Therefore, seven complete months are required: six for the
baseline and one current month.

Metrics currently evaluated:
- recognized sales;
- completed order count; and
- average order value.

The report now distinguishes three states:
- available: one or more anomalies were found;
- no_findings: the analysis completed and no metric crossed the threshold; and
- unavailable: the data or history was insufficient.

Design decisions:
- Median/MAD was selected over fixed percentage thresholds because it adapts
  to each business and is less distorted by large spikes.
- The robust threshold starts at 3.5 scaled deviations.
- An anomaly is an unusual movement, not a causal explanation.
- No anomaly result is generated when history is insufficient or periods are
  non-contiguous.
- An incomplete latest period is preserved as a friendly limitation message.
- The core detector has no LLM or RAG dependency.

Verification: ruff check, mypy, and the non-integration test suite passed
(147 passed, 4 deselected) on 2026-08-05.

MILESTONE 5 COMPLETION RECORD

Implemented in src/diagnostics/recommendations.py with tests in
tests/test_diagnostic_recommendations.py. The design decision is recorded in
docs/architecture/ADR-001-deterministic-recommendation-ranking.md.

The recommendation layer attaches only supported, human-reviewed investigation
actions to validated findings. It ranks them with an explicit score:

    ranking score = impact + urgency + confidence

Each component ranges from 0 to 3. High priority requires a score of at least
7; medium priority requires at least 4. Critical priority is intentionally not
used yet because the system lacks stronger evidence such as inventory, profit,
or operational-risk data.

Safeguards:
- Recommendations never execute a business change.
- Unsupported diagnostic types receive no generic recommendation.
- Recommendations do not claim a cause.
- Every recommendation requires human review.
- A two-period comparison is capped at medium priority because it is not a
  historical anomaly.

(162 passed, 4 deselected) on 2026-08-05.

BACKLOG: OPTIONAL LOCATION PERFORMANCE EXPLANATIONS

Branch, region, store, or location analysis must stay optional because many
restaurants and small businesses operate from one location or do not track
location in their CSV files.

When location data is available, the dashboard should avoid ranking locations
by revenue alone. It should explain location performance with supporting
signals such as order count, average order value, refunds, and discounts. This
keeps the analysis useful without implying that a high-revenue branch is
automatically the healthiest branch.

When location data is not available, the dashboard should show a friendly
capability message instead of an empty chart. For example: location performance
is unavailable because no branch, store, or region column was mapped.

BACKLOG: SCALABLE DATA-QUALITY ISSUE MANAGEMENT

When a CSV contains many invalid rows or issue groups, the dashboard should
remain readable and actionable. It should summarize issues by type and field,
show severity and affected-row counts, display only representative locations,
and provide filtering or expandable details for larger reports. The complete
set of affected rows should remain available through the downloadable
quarantine/correction file. This is a presentation and workflow improvement;
it must not weaken validation or hide the evidence needed to correct the data.

FORECAST EXPLANATION CORRECTION

The generic forecasting pipeline already compares multiple eligible transparent
methods through rolling historical backtests. The dashboard had shown only the
winning method, which could wrongly imply that it used one method alone.

The dashboard now states that the shown method was chosen after comparing
eligible methods by average historical error, and uses business-friendly names
for the methods. The full per-method comparison, trust label, and selection
reason are delivered in Milestone 7.

MILESTONE 11A COMPLETION RECORD

Implemented the product-demand data foundation without adding forecast values.
Line-item-capable uploads now support optional Product/SKU code, product name,
and unit-of-measure mappings. The canonical pipeline preserves those values, and
the new src/product_demand package provides typed assumptions, per-product
ready/limited/unavailable states, explicit reason codes, lifecycle-aware
fulfilled and returned units, and a deterministic daily product calendar.

The calendar distinguishes observed sales, confirmed zeroes, business closures,
known stockout-limited days, and missing/unknown days. It does not invent product
codes, unit conversions, unmet demand, or zeroes for ambiguous gaps. Order-total
mode is product-demand-ineligible, and a problem with one product restricts that
product rather than every valid series.

The official UCI Online Retail II workbook was profiled as a public real-world
challenge: 1,067,371 rows and 5,304 product codes were inspected. For the 25
busiest products, 14 remained limited because the source lacked stockout and
complete-export evidence, while 11 were unavailable because negative quantities
had no explicit cancellation/return meaning. This is an expected safety outcome,
not a failed test. It prevents the system from guessing whether those rows are
returns, corrections, or inventory adjustments.

deselected), all 3 Playwright Chromium journeys passed, frontend lint/build

MILESTONE 11B-1 APPROVED PLAN: BASELINE EVALUATION ENGINE

The four remaining design questions were resolved on 2026-08-31:
- negative source quantities never enter the fulfilled-demand target as negative
  demand; confirmed returns, cancellations, and inventory adjustments remain

- daily values must earn day-level evidence, with an evaluated seven-day total

  errors are measured separately without invented business-cost weights.

MILESTONE 11B-1 STEP 11 HISTORY-LENGTH SENSITIVITY RECORD
